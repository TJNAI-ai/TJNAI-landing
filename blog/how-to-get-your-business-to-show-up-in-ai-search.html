How to Get Your Business to Show Up in AI Search: What Google Actually Says

By TJNAI · November 3, 2026 · 9 min read

To get your business to show up in AI search, start with the same basics Google uses for regular search: claim and verify your Google Business Profile and keep it complete, make sure your website can be crawled and indexed, and publish helpful pages that only you could write. Google says there are no special AI-only requirements for AI Overviews or AI Mode. TJNAI's AI Agent Audit checks the parts of this that a business owner controls.

This guide sticks to what Google and OpenAI say on their own pages. Every rule below links to the source, so you can check it yourself.

What "AI search" means for a local business

AI search means search tools that write an answer for the person searching, such as Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, and ChatGPT search. Google's guidance names two things a local business controls: its Business Profile and its website.

AI Overviews and AI Mode in Google Search

AI Overviews and AI Mode are the AI features inside Google Search. Google says they may use a "query fan-out" technique, which runs related searches across subtopics to build one response (Google Search Central). AI Overviews don't appear on every search. Google says "AI Overviews are only shown when our systems determine that it is additive to classic Search, and as such, often don't trigger" (Google Search Central).

ChatGPT search and other AI tools

OpenAI says it uses a crawler called OAI-SearchBot for ChatGPT search (OpenAI). OpenAI says "OAI-SearchBot is used to surface websites in search results in ChatGPT's search features" (OpenAI). OpenAI also says "sites that are opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though can still appear as navigational links" (OpenAI). A site can allow OAI-SearchBot for search while blocking GPTBot, the crawler OpenAI uses for training (OpenAI).

Is "AI search optimization" different from SEO?

No. Google says "from Google Search's perspective, optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO" (Google Search Central). Google also says "there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary" (Google Search Central).

Google names several tactics you can skip:

  • Special AI files: "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search (including its generative AI capabilities), as Google Search itself doesn't use them" (Google Search Central). That includes files like llms.txt.
  • Chopping pages into pieces: "There's no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces for AI to better understand it" (Google Search Central).
  • Special schema: "Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add" (Google Search Central). Google still recommends structured data so a page can be eligible for rich results.
  • Bought mentions: Google says "seeking inauthentic 'mentions' across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem" (Google Search Central).

How to judge an "AEO" or "GEO" pitch

Judge an "AEO" or "GEO" pitch by whether it promises rankings or claims inside access to Google. Google's advice is to "be wary of third-party tools that promise ranking success or claim to use 'internal' Google metrics. No third-party tool has access to our internal ranking or AI systems" (Google Search Central). A fair pitch should describe the same basics Google lists: a complete Business Profile, a site Google can crawl, and useful pages.

Step 1: Claim, verify and complete your Google Business Profile

A complete, verified Google Business Profile is the first step, because Google ties Business Profile directly to its AI features. Google says "using products like Merchant Center (such as Merchant Center feeds) and Google Business Profiles can help your products and services to be visible in both AI responses and other Google Search results" (Google Search Central). Google's best-practice list for AI features includes "checking that your Merchant Center and Business Profile information is up-to-date" (Google Search Central).

Verification matters for local results too. Verifying "tells Google that you're authorized to represent the business, so it's more likely to show up in search results" (Google Business Profile Help). Google also says "businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results" (Google Business Profile Help).

Step 2: Make sure your website can be found and read

Your website has to be indexed and open to crawlers before Google's AI features can link to it. Google says "to be eligible to be shown as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet, fulfilling the Search technical requirements" (Google Search Central). Google adds that "indexing and serving isn't guaranteed" (Google Search Central).

Google's list also includes "ensuring that crawling is allowed in robots.txt, and by any CDN or hosting infrastructure" (Google Search Central).

To check your robots.txt, type your web address followed by /robots.txt into a browser, such as example.com/robots.txt. Look for lines that start with "Disallow". A line that reads "Disallow: /" under "User-agent: *" tells crawlers that follow robots.txt not to crawl any page on the site (Google Search Central). If you want to appear in ChatGPT search, make sure OAI-SearchBot isn't blocked, because OpenAI says sites opted out of OAI-SearchBot "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers" (OpenAI). If your site sits behind a CDN (a content delivery network) or your host has bot protection turned on, ask your web host whether those settings let search crawlers through.

  • Your main pages show up when you search Google for your business
  • robots.txt doesn't block your site
  • OAI-SearchBot is allowed, if you want to appear in ChatGPT search
  • Your host's or CDN's bot settings don't block search crawlers

Step 3: Write pages only you could write

Google says this is likely to matter more over time than anything else in its guide. In Google's words, "creating content that people find unique, compelling, and useful will likely influence your website's presence in generative AI search in the long run more than any of the other suggestions in this guide" (Google Search Central).

Google explains the difference with an example: "a first-hand review provides a unique perspective based on personal experience, whereas a summary of existing content simply restates information already available elsewhere" (Google Search Central). Google's May 2025 advice says the same: "focus on making unique, non-commodity content that visitors from Search and your own readers will find helpful and satisfying" (Google Search Central Blog).

For a local business, pages like these are a good place to start:

  • A page that answers the questions customers ask you on the phone
  • Your service area, in plain words
  • What a first visit or first job with you looks like

Our advice: one page per real question is enough. Google says making separate pages for every query variation "primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses in Google Search violates Google's scaled content abuse spam policy" (Google Search Central).

Step 4: Keep reviews coming and reply to them

Reviews help a business stand out in local results. Google says prominence "means how well-known a business is," that it's "also based on info like how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have," and that "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking" (Google Business Profile Help).

Google has firm rules on how you ask for reviews, including a ban on incentives. The review section of our Google Business Profile audit checklist covers those rules and how to reply.

How to check whether AI search is showing your business

Google Search Console has a report for this. Google says "the generative AI performance report shows data about how your site performs in generative AI features on Google Search," and it covers AI Overviews and AI Mode by page, country and device (Search Console Help).

A small site may not see the report yet. When the report doesn't appear, Google says "this usually happens when your site hasn't received enough impressions in generative AI features on Google Search" (Search Console Help).

Search Console also has a "Search generative AI control." Including your site is "the default control for all properties," so a site is included unless someone changes the setting (Search Console Help).

We also run a plain manual check, which is our own practice rather than a Google instruction. Ask Google and ChatGPT the questions your customers ask, such as the service you offer plus your town, and note whether your business and your website appear in the answers. Repeat the same questions every so often and compare.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a special "AI SEO" or "GEO" service to show up in AI Overviews?

No. Google says optimizing for generative AI search "is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO," and that there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode" (Google Search Central; Google's AI features guide). Google also warns that "no third-party tool has access to our internal ranking or AI systems" (Google Search Central).

Do I need an llms.txt file or special schema for AI search?

No, not for Google. Google says you don't need "AI text files, markup, or Markdown" because "Google Search itself doesn't use them," and that "there's no special schema.org markup you need to add" (Google Search Central).

Does my Google Business Profile affect AI search results?

Yes. Google says Business Profiles "can help your products and services to be visible in both AI responses and other Google Search results," and its best-practice list includes keeping Business Profile information up to date (Google Search Central; Google's AI features guide).

How do I get my business to show up in ChatGPT search?

Make sure your website doesn't block OAI-SearchBot, the crawler OpenAI says it uses "to surface websites in search results in ChatGPT's search features" (OpenAI). OpenAI says sites opted out of OAI-SearchBot "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers" (OpenAI).

How can I tell whether AI search is showing my business?

Check the generative AI performance report in Google Search Console, which "shows data about how your site performs in generative AI features on Google Search" (Search Console Help). If the report doesn't appear, Google says that usually means the site "hasn't received enough impressions in generative AI features" yet (Search Console Help).

Can I pay Google to rank higher in Maps or local results?

No. Google says "there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google" (Google Business Profile Help).
